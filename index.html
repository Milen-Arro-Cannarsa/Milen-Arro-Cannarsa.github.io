About

I am a monetary economist completing my PhD at the University of Bern under the supervision of Prof. Pierpaolo Benigno, and a research associate at the Study Center Gerzensee, the foundation of the Swiss National Bank, where I support the doctoral program and the courses for central bankers with exercise sessions and software training.

My research gives the safe-haven story a structural framework. In my job market paper, I develop an open-economy New Keynesian model in which foreign appetite for a country's liquid assets jointly produces appreciation, disinflation, and a liquidity trap of foreign origin, and in which the central bank's balance sheet becomes a second policy instrument. In plain terms, the paper answers two questions every Swiss institution lives with: why Swiss interest rates get stuck at low levels, and what moves the franc in a crisis.

The other half of my work is empirical. I co-authored an SNB working paper on machine-learning nowcasts of Swiss GDP. Before that, I spent a year in the SNB's business-cycle analysis unit, where I maintained the short-term forecasting models and carried sole responsibility for the foreign-trade forecasts for six months. Theory taught me how business cycles work, whereas forecasting made me fast and good with data.

Research

Job market paper · 2026

Flight to Liquidity: Monetary Policy in an Open Economy

Safe-haven economies experience global crises as a package: a surging currency, falling inflation, and a policy rate driven to its floor. I develop an open-economy New Keynesian model in which domestic assets carry a convenience yield because they provide liquidity services to the rest of the world. A flight to liquidity, matched to the franc's 2010–2011 appreciation, reproduces the joint dynamics of appreciation, disinflation, and a rate cut deep enough to hit the lower bound: a trap with a foreign origin, driven not by weak domestic demand but by the world's appetite for domestic assets. Supplying the liquid asset stabilizes the domestic economy exactly and removes the reason the trap arises, while a residual appreciation remains. Stabilizing the exchange rate instead imports an inflationary boom and requires an operation ten times larger, on the scale of the SNB balance sheet under the currency floor.

SNB Working Papers 2024-06 · Swiss National Bank

Nowcasting GDP: What Are the Gains from Machine Learning Algorithms?

with Rolf Scheufele

We compare penalized regressions (LASSO, ridge, elastic net), tree ensembles (bagging, random forest, gradient boosting), and support vector regression for nowcasting Swiss GDP, using a mixed-frequency data set of more than 1,100 series that accounts for publication lags. After the Great Recession, all machine-learning methods beat the univariate benchmark by up to 28% in out-of-sample RMSE; ridge, elastic net, and SVR perform best.
