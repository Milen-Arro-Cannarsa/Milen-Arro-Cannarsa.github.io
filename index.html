About

I am a monetary economist completing my PhD at the University of Bern under the supervision of Prof. Pierpaolo Benigno.

I work on the design of monetary policy in open economies: interest-rate and balance-sheet policy, foreign exchange intervention, and convenience yields on safe assets. My job market paper asks what happens to an economy when the rest of the world wants its assets. A flight to safety raises the convenience yield at home and tightens monetary conditions at an unchanged policy rate, which can push the economy into a liquidity trap of foreign origin. The central bank’s balance sheet becomes a second instrument. The paper received the 2026 Bank of Estonia Research Award in the doctoral category. In current work, I study optimal monetary policy for the issuer of a reserve currency.

The other half of my work is empirical: an SNB working paper on machine-learning nowcasts of Swiss GDP, and a year in the SNB’s business-cycle analysis unit, where I maintained the short-term forecasting models and was responsible for the foreign-trade forecasts for six months.

Research

Job market paper · 2026

Flight to Safety: Monetary Policy in an Open Economy

Winner of the 2026 Bank of Estonia Research Award (doctoral category)

When investors seek safety, the countries whose assets they buy can pay a price. I show that a flight to safety raises the convenience yield on domestic assets. This tightens monetary conditions at home with the policy rate unchanged, and can push the economy into a liquidity trap of foreign origin. The central bank can offset the tightening by supplying the asset the world wants. For Switzerland in the acute phase of the Global Financial Crisis, the operation is 7% of annual GDP, close to what the SNB bought at the time.

SNB Working Papers 2024-06 · Swiss National Bank

Nowcasting GDP: What Are the Gains from Machine Learning Algorithms?

with Rolf Scheufele

We compare penalized regressions (LASSO, ridge, elastic net), tree ensembles (bagging, random forest, gradient boosting), and support vector regression for nowcasting Swiss GDP, using a mixed-frequency data set of more than 1,100 series that accounts for publication lags. After the Great Recession, all machine-learning methods beat the univariate benchmark by up to 28% in out-of-sample RMSE; ridge, elastic net, and SVR perform best.

Work in progress

Selling Safety: Optimal Monetary Policy for a Reserve Currency

I study how the issuer of a reserve currency should set the policy rate and the supply of its safe asset. The asset gives services to residents and earns income from abroad, so its premium is something to price, not remove. I characterize the optimal response of both instruments to a surge in foreign demand for the asset.
